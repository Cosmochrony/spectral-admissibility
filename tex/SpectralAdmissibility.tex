  \begin{abstract}
    % >>> Begin input: abstract.tex
We present the first analytically tractable instance of the spectral admissibility
programme proposed in the Cosmochrony framework.
On the undirected Cayley graph of the quaternion group $Q_8$, with generators
$S=\{\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}$, a local bounded–flux constraint
$|\partial_t\chi_v|\le c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ induces a hierarchy of maximal admissible
mode amplitudes across irreducible spectral sectors.

In the linearised regime, the admissibility bound takes the universal form
\[
  A_n^{\max}=\frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}},
\]
where $\lambda_n$ are the Laplacian eigenvalues of the graph.
This produces a spectral hierarchy in which the non-abelian sector
admits a larger maximal effective amplitude than the nontrivial abelian sectors
by a factor $\sqrt{4/3}$.

We then perform a weakly nonlinear modal reduction of the discrete
Dirac–Born–Infeld dynamics and show that the first nonlinear corrections
preserve this hierarchy.
Solving the one-mode DBI dynamics exactly on the $Q_8$ graph, we further show
that the same envelope $A_n^{\max}=c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ coincides with the
exact saturation boundary, demonstrating structural stability beyond the
perturbative regime.

The analysis is extended to the binary icosahedral group $2I$, the largest finite
subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, where the same admissibility envelope generates
a four-level hierarchy across spin sectors with contrast $\sqrt{14/9}$.
Together these results provide explicit realizations of spectral admissibility
along the chain
\[
  Q_8 \subset 2I \subset \mathrm{SU}(2).
\]

The analysis is further extended to the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ chain, following the exact parallel
structure.
On the minimal $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-type group $\Delta(27) = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)$, the
tracelessness mechanism produces $\mu_{\mathbf{3}} = 0$ for the Weil representation, establishing
the structural anchor of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ hierarchy.
On the group $\Sigma(168) = \mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ of order~$168$, the analogue of $2I$ for
$\mathrm{SU}(3)$, a complete character-theoretic computation for the canonical generating set
$S = C_4$ (the $42$ elements of order~$4$) yields
\[
  \lambda_{\mathbf{3}} = \lambda_{\bar{\mathbf{3}}} = 28
  \;<\;
  \lambda_{\mathbf{6}} = \lambda_{\mathbf{8}} = 42
  \;<\;
  \lambda_{\mathbf{7}} = 48,
\]
with admissibility contrast $\sqrt{12/7} \approx 1.309$.
This establishes that the fundamental and anti-fundamental representations of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
are co-admissible and strictly dominate all other non-trivial sectors, in exact analogy with the
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ result on $2I$.
The algebraic origin is the character identity $\chi_{\mathbf{3}}(g) = 1$ for all $g \in C_4$,
the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ counterpart of the golden-ratio identity on $2I$.

More generally, the results show that bounded substrate capacity can act as a
purely spectral filter favouring low-lying non-abelian sectors of relational
dynamics, and that this mechanism operates uniformly across the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ chains.

\section{Setting and Motivation}

  The Cosmochrony framework identifies the physical spectrum with the residue of two
  successive constraints acting on the relational operator $\mathcal{L}_\chi$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Topological discretisation}: the fibre structure of the non-injective
    projection $\Pi$ organises modes into discrete winding sectors.
    \item \textbf{Bounded-flux filtering}: the saturation bound
    $|\partial_t \chi| \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ eliminates modes whose local evolution would
    exceed the substrate's relational capacity.
  \end{enumerate}
  The goal of this note is to make step~2 explicit and calculable on the simplest
  non-trivial finite graphs encoding quaternionic and colour algebraic structures,
  as minimal discrete proxies for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$- and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-type sectors.

\section{The $Q_8$ Cayley Graph}
  \label{sec:q8-}

  Let $Q_8 = \{{\pm 1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k}\}$ denote the quaternion group of order~8.
  We define the undirected Cayley graph $\Gamma(Q_8, S)$ with generating set
  \begin{equation}
    S = \{\pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}.
  \end{equation}
  Since $S$ is closed under inversion ($s^{-1} \in S$ for all $s \in S$) and does
  not contain the identity, $\Gamma(Q_8, S)$ is a well-defined, connected, undirected,
  $6$-regular graph on $8$ vertices.

  \begin{remark}
    The choice $S = \{i, j, k\}$ without inverses would yield a directed graph with an
    asymmetric adjacency operator.
    For a self-adjoint Laplacian, the full symmetric set
    $S = \{\pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$ is required.
    The resulting graph is $6$-regular, not $3$-regular.
  \end{remark}

\subsection{Adjacency Operator and Graph Laplacian}
  \label{subsec:adjacency-operator-graph-laplacian}

  The adjacency operator $A$ of $\Gamma(Q_8, S)$ acts on functions
  $f \colon Q_8 \to \mathbb{C}$ by
  \begin{equation}
    (Af)(g) = \sum_{s \in S} f(gs)
  = \sum_{s \in \{\pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}} f(gs).
  \end{equation}
  The combinatorial graph Laplacian is
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = dI - A,
    \qquad
    d = |S| = 6,
  \end{equation}
  where $I$ is the identity on $\ell^2(Q_8)$.
  The eigenvalues of $\mathcal{L}$ are $\lambda = 6 - \mu$, where $\mu$ ranges over
  the eigenvalues of $A$.

\section{Spectral Decomposition via Representation Theory}
  \label{sec:spectral-decomposition}

  Since $Q_8$ is a finite group, $\ell^2(Q_8) \cong \mathbb{C}[Q_8]$ decomposes
  as a $Q_8$-module into irreducible representations.
  By the Peter--Weyl theorem for finite groups:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbb{C}[Q_8] \cong \bigoplus_{\rho \in \widehat{Q_8}} V_\rho^{\oplus \dim \rho},
  \end{equation}
  where the sum runs over the five irreducible representations of $Q_8$.

\subsection{Eigenvalues of the Adjacency Operator}
  \label{subsec:eigenvalues-of-adjacency-operator}

  For any irreducible representation $\rho$, the adjacency operator acts as the
  scalar $\mu_\rho \cdot I_{V_\rho}$ on $V_\rho$.
  Since $S$ is a union of conjugacy classes of $Q_8$, the corresponding element
  of the group algebra is central, and Schur's lemma gives
  \begin{equation}
    \mu_\rho = \frac{1}{\dim \rho} \sum_{s \in S} \chi_\rho(s),
  \end{equation}
  where $\chi_\rho$ is the character of $\rho$.

  \paragraph{1D sectors.}
    For the trivial representation $\rho_0$, $\chi_{\rho_0}(s) = 1$ for all
    $s \in S$, so $\sum_{s \in S} \chi_{\rho_0}(s) = 6$ and
    \begin{equation}
      \mu_{\rho_0} = 6,
      \qquad
      \lambda_{\rho_0} = 6 - 6 = 0.
    \end{equation}
    For $\rho_1$, $\chi_{\rho_1}(\pm i) = 1$ and
    $\chi_{\rho_1}(\pm j) = \chi_{\rho_1}(\pm k) = -1$,
    so $\sum_{s \in S} \chi_{\rho_1}(s) = 2(1) + 4(-1) = -2$, giving
    $\mu_{\rho_1} = -2$ and $\lambda_{\rho_1} = 8$.
    By the symmetry of $Q_8$ under permutations of $\{i,j,k\}$,
    $\mu_{\rho_2} = \mu_{\rho_3} = -2$ and
    $\lambda_{\rho_2} = \lambda_{\rho_3} = 8$.

  \paragraph{2D sector.}
    For the quaternionic representation $\rho_4$, we compute the character at each
    generator.
    The matrices $\rho_4(i)$, $\rho_4(-i)$, $\rho_4(j)$, $\rho_4(-j)$,
    $\rho_4(k)$, $\rho_4(-k)$ each have trace zero.
    Thus $\sum_{s \in S} \chi_{\rho_4}(s) = 0$ and
    \begin{equation}
      \mu_{\rho_4} = 0,
      \qquad
      \lambda_{\rho_4} = 6.
    \end{equation}

\subsection{Setup}
  \label{subsec:setup}

  Let the substrate field $\chi \colon Q_8 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ evolve
  on the graph, subject to the local bounded-flux constraint
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:flux-bound}
    |\partial_t \chi_v(t)| \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}
    \qquad
    \forall v \in Q_8,
    \ \forall t.
  \end{equation}
  Consider a single-mode excitation
  \begin{equation}
    \chi_v(t) = a_n \psi_n(v) \cos(\omega_n t),
  \end{equation}
  where $\psi_n$ is an eigenmode of $\mathcal{L}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$,
  normalised in $\ell^2(Q_8)$, and $a_n > 0$ is the mode amplitude.
  The time derivative at vertex $v$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \partial_t \chi_v(t) = -a_n \omega_n \psi_n(v) \sin(\omega_n t).
  \end{equation}
  The constraint~\eqref{eq:flux-bound} requires
  \begin{equation}
    |\omega_n| \cdot a_n \cdot |\psi_n(v)| \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}
    \qquad
    \forall v \in Q_8.
  \end{equation}
  The binding constraint is at the vertex where $|\psi_n(v)|$ is maximal:
  \begin{equation}
    |\omega_n| \cdot a_n \cdot \|\psi_n\|_\infty \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}.
  \end{equation}

\subsection{Effective Amplitude and Admissibility Envelope}
  \label{subsec:effective-amplitude-and-admissibility-envelope}

  \begin{definition}
    The effective amplitude of a mode $(\psi_n, a_n)$ is
    \begin{equation}
      A_n := a_n \|\psi_n\|_\infty.
    \end{equation}
    A mode is dynamically admissible in the linearised regime if
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:admissibility}
      |\omega_n| \cdot A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}.
    \end{equation}
  \end{definition}

  In the linearised dispersion approximation, $\omega_n^2 \approx \lambda_n$, so the
  maximal admissible effective amplitude is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:envelope}
    A_n^{\max} = \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}.
  \end{equation}
  This defines the admissibility envelope in the $(\lambda_n, A_n)$ plane.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{../code/fig1_Q8_admissibility}
    \caption{
      Admissibility envelope on the $Q_8$ Cayley graph with
      $S=\{\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}$.
      The maximal effective amplitude
      $A_n^{\max}=c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$
      is larger in the quaternionic non-abelian sector
      $\rho_4$ ($\lambda=6$) than in the non-trivial abelian sectors
      ($\lambda=8$), by the factor $\sqrt{4/3}$.
    }
    \label{fig:q8-admissibility}
  \end{figure}

  \begin{remark}
    The linearised dispersion $\omega_n^2 = \lambda_n$ is valid for
    $A_n \ll c_{\mathrm{BI}} / \sqrt{\lambda_n}$.
    The non-linear Born--Infeld correction modifies this relation near saturation and
    requires a modal reduction of the DBI action.
    The envelope~\eqref{eq:envelope} should therefore be understood first as the
    admissibility boundary in the weakly non-linear regime.
  \end{remark}

\subsection{Explicit Eigenmodes in the $\rho_4$ Sector}
  \label{subsec:explicit-rho4}

  The non-abelian sector $\rho_4$ has multiplicity $4$ in $\ell^2(Q_8)$, so the
  quantity $\|\psi_n\|_\infty$ is not determined until an explicit basis of the
  eigenspace is chosen.
  A natural orthonormal basis is provided by the matrix coefficients of the
  quaternionic irreducible representation.

  For a finite group $G$ and an irreducible representation $\rho$, the standard
  Peter--Weyl basis of the corresponding isotypic component is given by
  \begin{equation}
    \psi_{uv}(g)
    =
    \sqrt{\frac{\dim \rho}{|G|}}\,\rho(g)_{uv},
    \qquad
    u,v \in \{1,\dots,\dim \rho\}.
  \end{equation}
  For $Q_8$ and $\rho_4$, this becomes
  \begin{equation}
    \psi_{uv}(g)
    =
    \frac{1}{2}\,\rho_4(g)_{uv},
    \qquad
    u,v \in \{1,2\},
  \end{equation}
  since $\dim \rho_4 = 2$ and $|Q_8| = 8$.

  Using the explicit matrices
  \begin{equation}
    \rho_4(1)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      1 & 0 \\
      0 & 1
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \rho_4(-1)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      -1 & 0 \\
      0 & -1
    \end{pmatrix},
  \end{equation}
  \begin{equation}
    \rho_4(i)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      i & 0 \\
      0 & -i
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \rho_4(j)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      0 & 1 \\
      -1 & 0
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \rho_4(k)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
      0 & i \\
      i & 0
    \end{pmatrix},
  \end{equation}
  one finds that the diagonal modes $\psi_{11}, \psi_{22}$ are supported on
  $\{\pm 1, \pm i\}$, while the off-diagonal modes $\psi_{12}, \psi_{21}$ are
  supported on $\{\pm j, \pm k\}$.
  In all four cases,
  \begin{equation}
    |\psi_{uv}(g)| \in \left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\right\},
  \end{equation}
  hence
  \begin{equation}
    \|\psi_{uv}\|_\infty = \frac{1}{2},
    \qquad
    u,v \in \{1,2\}.
  \end{equation}

  For the one-dimensional nontrivial abelian sectors $\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3$,
  the normalised eigenmodes are the corresponding characters scaled by
  $|Q_8|^{-1/2}$.
  Since these characters take values in $\{\pm 1\}$, one has
  \begin{equation}
    \|\psi_{\rho_k}\|_\infty = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8}},
    \qquad
    k = 1,2,3.
  \end{equation}

\subsection{Basis-Resolved Admissibility}
  \label{subsec:basis-resolved-admissibility}

  \begin{proposition}[Basis-resolved admissibility on $Q_8$]
    \label{prop:basis-resolved}
    In the canonical Peter--Weyl basis of $\ell^2(Q_8)$, the maximal admissible
    effective amplitudes satisfy
    \begin{equation}
      A_{\rho_4}^{\max}
      =
      \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{6}}
      >
      \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{8}}
      =
      A_{\rho_k}^{\max},
      \qquad
      k=1,2,3,
    \end{equation}
    whereas the corresponding maximal raw amplitudes satisfy
    \begin{equation}
      a_{\rho_4}^{\max}
      =
      \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{6}\,\|\psi_{\rho_4}\|_\infty}
      =
      \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\,c_{\mathrm{BI}}
      \approx 0.816\,c_{\mathrm{BI}},
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
      a_{\rho_k}^{\max}
      =
      \frac{c_{\mathrm{BI}}}{\sqrt{8}\,\|\psi_{\rho_k}\|_\infty}
      =
      c_{\mathrm{BI}},
      \qquad
      k=1,2,3.
    \end{equation}
    Thus the non-abelian sector is favoured in effective amplitude, but not in raw
    amplitude.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    The effective-amplitude statement follows from
    $A_n^{\max} = c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ together with
    $\lambda_{\rho_4} = 6$ and $\lambda_{\rho_k} = 8$.
    The raw-amplitude statement follows from
    $a_n^{\max} = A_n^{\max}/\|\psi_n\|_\infty$, using
    $\|\psi_{\rho_4}\|_\infty = 1/2$ and
    $\|\psi_{\rho_k}\|_\infty = 1/\sqrt{8}$.
  \end{proof}

\subsection{Weakly Non-linear Modal DBI Reduction}
  \label{subsec:weakly-nonlinear-dbi-reduction}

  To incorporate the first Born--Infeld correction, consider the discrete DBI Lagrangian
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:dbi-lagrangian}
    L
    =
    -c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2 \sum_v \sqrt{1 - \dot{\chi}_v^2/c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
    - \tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{v,w} \chi_v \mathcal{L}_{vw} \chi_w.
  \end{equation}
  For a single eigenmode,
  \begin{equation}
    \chi_v(t) = q(t)\psi_n(v),
  \end{equation}
  with $\mathcal{L}\psi_n = \lambda_n \psi_n$ and $\|\psi_n\|_2 = 1$, this reduces to
  the exact one-mode Lagrangian
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:exact-mode-lagrangian}
    L_n[q]
    =
    -c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2 \sum_v \sqrt{1 - \psi_n(v)^2 \dot{q}^{\,2}/c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_n q^2.
  \end{equation}

  Expanding the square root for $|\dot{q}| \ll c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ gives
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:mode-lagrangian-expansion}
    L_n[q]
    =
    \mathrm{const}
    + \tfrac{1}{2}\dot{q}^{\,2}
    + \frac{\kappa_n}{8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}\dot{q}^{\,4}
    - \tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_n q^2
    + \mathcal{O}(c_{\mathrm{BI}}^{-4}),
  \end{equation}
  where
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:kappa-def}
    \kappa_n := \sum_v \psi_n(v)^4.
  \end{equation}
  The corresponding Euler--Lagrange equation is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:weakly-nonlinear-mode-equation}
    \left(
      1 + \frac{3\kappa_n}{2c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}\dot{q}^{\,2}
    \right)\ddot{q}
    + \lambda_n q
    = 0.
  \end{equation}

  Using the harmonic-balance ansatz
  \begin{equation}
    q(t) \approx a_n \cos(\omega_n t),
  \end{equation}
  and keeping only the fundamental harmonic, one obtains the first
  amplitude-dependent correction
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:omega-shift-square}
    \omega_n^2(a_n)
    \approx
    \lambda_n
    \left(
      1 - \frac{3\kappa_n\lambda_n a_n^2}{8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
    \right),
  \end{equation}
  or equivalently
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:omega-shift}
    \omega_n(a_n)
    \approx
    \sqrt{\lambda_n}
    \left(
      1 - \frac{3\kappa_n\lambda_n a_n^2}{16c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
    \right).
  \end{equation}
  The DBI correction is therefore a softening non-linearity.

  In the canonical $Q_8$ basis, the quartic shape factor is explicit.
  For the one-dimensional nontrivial abelian sectors,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:kappa-abelian}
    \kappa_{\rho_k}
    =
    \sum_{v \in Q_8} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}\right)^4
    =
    \frac{1}{8},
    \qquad
    k = 1,2,3.
  \end{equation}
  For each canonical basis vector in the $\rho_4$ sector,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:kappa-nonabelian}
    \kappa_{\rho_4}
    =
    4 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^4
    =
    \frac{1}{4}.
  \end{equation}

  However, rewriting the correction in terms of the effective amplitude
  $A_n = a_n\|\psi_n\|_\infty$ gives
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:omega-shift-effective}
    \omega_n^2(A_n)
    \approx
    \lambda_n
    \left(
      1
      - \frac{3\lambda_n A_n^2}{8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
      \frac{\kappa_n}{\|\psi_n\|_\infty^2}
    \right).
  \end{equation}
  In the canonical $Q_8$ basis,
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{\kappa_{\rho_k}}{\|\psi_{\rho_k}\|_\infty^2}
    =
    \frac{(1/8)}{(1/\sqrt{8})^2}
    =
    1,
    \qquad
    \frac{\kappa_{\rho_4}}{\|\psi_{\rho_4}\|_\infty^2}
    =
    \frac{(1/4)}{(1/2)^2}
    =
    1.
  \end{equation}
  Therefore
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:omega-shift-effective-q8}
    \omega_n^2(A_n)
    \approx
    \lambda_n
    \left(
      1 - \frac{3\lambda_n A_n^2}{8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}
    \right)
  \end{equation}
  in both the nontrivial abelian and non-abelian sectors.

\subsection{Non-perturbative DBI Envelope}

  The weakly non-linear reduction derived in

  Eq.~\eqref{eq:dbi-lagrangian} gives the exact one-mode Lagrangian
  \begin{equation}
    L_n[q]
    =
    -c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2
    \sum_{v\in Q_8}

    -
    \frac12\lambda_n q^2 .

      \qquad

      L
      =
      -8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2
      \sqrt{1-\frac{\dot q^{\,2}}{8c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2}}
      -
      \frac12\lambda_n q^2 .
    \end{equation}

      |\psi_n(v)| =

        \frac12 & \text{on four vertices}, \\
        0 & \text{on four vertices}.
      \end{cases}

      -

      |\partial_t \chi_v| \le c_{\mathrm{BI}} .

\section{Generating Set for the LPS Family}
  \label{sec:step6a}

  The spectral admissibility programme follows the chain
  \begin{equation}
    Q_8
    \;\longrightarrow\;
    2I
    \;\longrightarrow\;
    \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{F}_p)
    \;\xrightarrow{\;p \to \infty\;}
    \mathrm{SU}(2),
    \label{eq:chain-6a}
  \end{equation}
  where the final arrow denotes local spectral convergence toward the
  $S^3$ Laplacian.
  The first concrete step of the third stage is an explicit symmetric
  generating set whose structural role is analogous to the order-4
  generators of $Q_8$ and the order-10 generators of $2I$.
  This section constructs such a set via the Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak
  (LPS) construction~\cite{LPS1988}.

  \subsection{Quaternion algebra and the spinor embedding}
    \label{sec:step6a-quat}

    The Hurwitz quaternion algebra $\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Z})$ embeds into
    $M_2(\mathbb{F}_q)$ via the standard spinor map.
    Fix a square root of $-1$ in $\mathbb{F}_q$, denoted $\mathbf{i}_q$
    (which exists since $q \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$).
    The map
    \begin{equation}
      \Phi_q \colon \mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow M_2(\mathbb{F}_q),
      \qquad
      a + b\mathbf{i} + c\mathbf{j} + d\mathbf{k}
      \;\longmapsto\;
      \begin{pmatrix}
        a + b\,\mathbf{i}_q & c + d\,\mathbf{i}_q \\
        -c + d\,\mathbf{i}_q & a - b\,\mathbf{i}_q
      \end{pmatrix}
      \pmod{q}
      \label{eq:spinor-map}
    \end{equation}
    sends a quaternion of integer norm $a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2$ to a matrix of the
    same determinant modulo $q$.
    For a quaternion of norm $p$, one has
    $\det \Phi_q(\cdot) \equiv p \pmod{q}$.
    To normalise the determinant to $1$, one multiplies by $r^{-1} \in
\mathbb{F}_q$ where $r$ satisfies $r^2 \equiv p \pmod{q}$.
    Such $r$ exists provided $p$ is a quadratic residue modulo $q$.

    \begin{remark}
      The normalisation factor $r^{-1}$ is an element of $\mathbb{F}_q$,
      not of $\mathbb{F}_p$.
      There is no division by $\sqrt{p}$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$; the entire
      construction takes place in $\mathbb{F}_q$.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The LPS generating set}
    \label{sec:step6a-lps}

    \begin{definition}[LPS generators~\cite{LPS1988}]
      \label{def:lps}
      Let $p, q$ be distinct primes with $p \equiv q \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ and
      $\bigl(\frac{p}{q}\bigr) = 1$.
      Fix $\mathbf{i}_q$ with $\mathbf{i}_q^2 \equiv -1 \pmod{q}$, and
      $r \in \mathbb{F}_q$ with $r^2 \equiv p \pmod{q}$.
      Let $\Omega_p$ be the set of integer solutions to
      \begin{equation}
        a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2 = p,
        \qquad a > 0,\; a \text{ odd},\; b, c, d \text{ even}.
        \label{eq:sum-of-4-squares}
      \end{equation}
      By Jacobi's four-square theorem, after quotienting by sign and permutation
      symmetries, there are exactly $p+1$ such solutions.
      For each $(a,b,c,d) \in \Omega_p$, define
      \begin{equation}
        g_{(a,b,c,d)}
        = r^{-1}
        \begin{pmatrix}
          a + b\,\mathbf{i}_q & c + d\,\mathbf{i}_q \\
          -c + d\,\mathbf{i}_q & a - b\,\mathbf{i}_q
        \end{pmatrix}
        \pmod{q}
        \;\in\; \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q).
        \label{eq:lps-generator}
      \end{equation}
      The LPS generating set is
      \begin{equation}
        S_p = \bigl\{ \pm\, g_{(a,b,c,d)} \mid (a,b,c,d) \in \Omega_p \bigr\}
        \big/\{\pm I\}
        \;\subset\; \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q),
        \qquad |S_p| = p+1.
        \label{eq:S-SL2}
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    The set $S_p$ is symmetric ($g \in S_p \Rightarrow g^{-1} \in S_p$)
    because $(-a,-b,-c,-d) \in \Omega_p$ whenever $(a,b,c,d) \in \Omega_p$,
    and $g_{(-a,-b,-c,-d)} = g_{(a,b,c,d)}^{-1}$.
    The Cayley graph $X^{p,q} = \mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q), S_p)$
    is therefore undirected and the associated Laplacian self-adjoint.

  \subsection{The Ramanujan property}
    \label{sec:step6a-ramanujan}

    The graph $X^{p,q}$ is a $(p+1)$-regular Ramanujan graph~\cite{LPS1988}.
    This means that all non-trivial adjacency eigenvalues $\mu$
    (i.e.\ all except the trivial eigenvalue $\mu = p+1$) satisfy
    \begin{equation}
      |\mu| \leq 2\sqrt{p},
      \label{eq:ramanujan-bound}
    \end{equation}
    which saturates the Alon--Boppana lower bound on the spectral gap.
    Equivalently, all non-trivial Laplacian eigenvalues satisfy
    \begin{equation}
      \lambda = (p+1) - \mu \geq \bigl(\sqrt{p} - 1\bigr)^2 > 0.
      \label{eq:ramanujan-gap}
    \end{equation}
    The admissibility envelope $A^{\max}_\rho = c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_\rho}$
    therefore remains well-defined and bounded away from infinity for every
    non-trivial representation $\rho$, uniformly for all $q$.

    The proof uses the Ramanujan--Petersson conjecture for holomorphic cusp
    forms of weight~2, established by Eichler and Shimura.
    The sum-of-four-squares condition~\eqref{eq:sum-of-4-squares} is what
    connects the adjacency operator of $X^{p,q}$ to Hecke operators on
    modular forms, whose eigenvalues are controlled by the Ramanujan bound.

  \subsection{The normalised character correlation: key observable}
    \label{sec:step6a-coherence}

    With $S_p$ defined, the key observable for each low-dimensional
    representation sector $\rho$ of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)$ is the
    \emph{normalised character correlation}
    \begin{equation}
      \kappa_\rho \;\equiv\; \frac{\mu_\rho}{p+1}
      = \frac{1}{(p+1)\dim\rho} \sum_{g \in S_p} \chi_\rho(g).
      \label{eq:coherence}
    \end{equation}
    As a guiding heuristic, the low-dimensional representations of
    $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)$ play the role of discrete analogues of
    the low-spin representations of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ in the large-$q$ limit.
    We index them by $j$ accordingly, writing $\kappa_j$ for $\kappa_{\rho_j}$,
    with the understanding that the identification with $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ spins
    is an asymptotic guide, not a formal equivalence at finite $q$.
    The quantity $\kappa_j$ measures the coherence between $S_p$ and the
    representation $\rho_j$ per generator per dimension.

    The results of Steps~4 and~5 already show that $\kappa_j$ decreases
    with $j$ in the two smallest groups:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item On $Q_8$: $\kappa_{1/2} = 0$ and $\kappa_1 = -1/3 < 0$
      (Pauli tracelessness forces $\mu_{\rho_4} = 0$ exactly).
      \item On $2I$: $\kappa_{1/2} > \kappa_1 > \kappa_{3/2} \geq \kappa_2$
      (the golden ratio identity controls the two lowest levels, and the
      hierarchy extends upward).
    \end{itemize}
    The structural reason is the behaviour of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ characters
    \begin{equation}
      \chi_j(\theta) = \frac{\sin\bigl((2j+1)\theta/2\bigr)}{\sin(\theta/2)},
      \label{eq:SU2-character}
    \end{equation}
    which oscillate increasingly rapidly as $j$ grows.
    For the LPS generators, which are concentrated at a definite conjugacy
    class in $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)$, the sum $\sum_{g \in S_p}
\chi_j(g)$ experiences growing cancellations as $j$ increases, because
    the numerator in~\eqref{eq:SU2-character} oscillates while the
    denominator does not.
    The normalised correlation $\kappa_j$ therefore tends to decrease with
    $j$, pushing $\lambda_j$ upward and the admissibility window
    $A^{\max}_j$ downward for large spin.

    The central prediction of Step~6 is that this hierarchy extends to all
    members of the LPS family: for every $p$, the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector
    remains the most admissible.
    This is established numerically in Steps~6b--6d and summarised in
    Section~\ref{sec:step6bcd}.

    As $q \to \infty$ with $p$ fixed, the Cayley graph $X^{p,q}$ converges
    locally to the $(p+1)$-regular tree, and its spectral measure converges
    to the Kesten--McKay measure supported on $[(\sqrt{p}-1)^2,(\sqrt{p}+1)^2]$.
    In this limit the individual eigenvalues fill the interval densely, and
    the natural spectral sectors are labelled by the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ spin
    $j \in \{0, \tfrac{1}{2}, 1, \tfrac{3}{2}, \ldots\}$, exactly as in the
    $Q_8$ and $2I$ analyses.

    \paragraph{LPS generators as $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ elements.}
      Each $(a,b,c,d) \in \Omega_p$ defines a unit quaternion
    \begin{equation}
      g_{(a,b,c,d)} = \frac{a + b\,\mathbf{i} + c\,\mathbf{j} + d\,\mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{p}}
      \;\in\; \mathrm{SU}(2).
      \label{eq:lps-su2-element}
      \end{equation}
      Its $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ trace is $2a/\sqrt{p}$, so the eigenvalues are
      $e^{\pm i\phi}$ where

      \phi = \arccos\!\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{p}}\right).
      \label{eq:lps-angle}
      \end{equation}
      The character of the spin-$j$ representation at this element is
    \begin{equation}
      \chi_j\!\left(g_{(a,b,c,d)}\right)

    The Ramanujan property of $X^{p,q}$ gives uniform control on both ends

    \begin{proposition}[Ramanujan bound on the admissibility ratio]
      \label{prop:ramanujan-R}
      For any Ramanujan graph of degree $d = p+1$, the non-trivial Laplacian
      eigenvalues satisfy
      \begin{equation}
        \left(\sqrt{p} - 1\right)^2 \;\leq\; \lambda \;\leq\; \left(\sqrt{p}+1\right)^2.
        \label{eq:ramanujan-window}
      \end{equation}
      Consequently,
      \begin{equation}
        1 \;<\; R_p \;\leq\; \frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{\sqrt{p}-1}.
        \label{eq:ramanujan-R-bound}
      \end{equation}
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The lower bound on $\lambda$ is the Alon--Boppana theorem; saturation for
      Ramanujan graphs is the LPS result~\cite{LPS1988}.
      The upper bound on $R_p$ follows by taking the ratio of the extreme values.
      The strict lower bound $R_p > 1$ is Proposition~\ref{prop:lps-hierarchy}.
    \end{proof}

  \paragraph{Asymptotic behaviour.}
    As $p \to \infty$, the Ramanujan bound gives
    \begin{equation}
      \frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{\sqrt{p}-1}
      = 1 + \frac{2}{\sqrt{p}-1}
      \;\xrightarrow{p\to\infty}\; 1.
      \label{eq:R-bound-limit}
    \end{equation}

    ratio $R_p$ is squeezed toward $1$ from above.

    behaviour, with $R_p$ oscillating in the interval $(1, 1.75)$ for the range
    $5 \leq p \leq 89$.

    In the $\mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3$ continuum, the Laplacian spectrum is
    $\ell(\ell+2)$ with uniform spectral density: there is no preferred spin
    sector, and the admissibility ratio is $R = 1$.
    The LPS hierarchy is a finite-group effect that reflects the discrete
    structure of the generating set.
    As $p$ grows, the graph becomes a progressively finer approximation to
    $S^3$, and the hierarchy attenuates accordingly.
    The spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ advantage persists at every finite stage but is
    reabsorbed into the continuous symmetry in the limit.

filter disappears — consistently with the expectation that a continuous
relational substrate admits all spin sectors on equal terms.
The discrete hierarchy is therefore not a defect of the finite
approximations but a genuine structural feature of the bounded-flux
mechanism at finite resolution.

    \subsubsection{Group structure and representations}

    The group $\Delta(27) = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)$ is the Heisenberg group of
    upper-triangular $3 \times 3$ unipotent matrices over $\mathbb{F}_3$, of order~$27$.
    Its centre is $Z \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, the commutator subgroup coincides with
    the centre, and $\Delta(27)/Z \cong \mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$.

    The number of irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes.
    Since $[G,G] = Z$ has order~$3$, the group has $|G/[G,G]| = 9$ one-dimensional
    characters.
    The remaining dimensions are fixed by $\sum_\rho \dim(\rho)^2 = |G| = 27$: with
    $9 \times 1^2 = 9$ accounted for, the remaining $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ degrees require
    exactly two three-dimensional representations.
    The full irrep content is therefore
    \begin{equation}
      \underbrace{1^{\times 9}}_{\text{abelian}} \oplus
      \underbrace{3,\,\bar{3}}_{\text{Weil pair}},
      \qquad
      9 \times 1^2 + 2 \times 3^2 = 27 \;\checkmark.
      \label{eq:delta27-irreps}
    \end{equation}
    The two three-dimensional representations are the Weil representation $\mathbf{3}$ and
    its complex conjugate $\bar{\mathbf{3}}$.

    \subsubsection{Generating set and the $\mu_{\mathbf{3}} = 0$ identity}

    The standard generating set is
    \begin{equation}
      S_{27} = \{X^{\pm 1},\, Y^{\pm 1},\, (XY)^{\pm 1}\},
      \qquad
      |S_{27}| = 6,
      \label{eq:S27}
    \end{equation}
    where $X, Y$ are the canonical generators of $\Delta(27)$ satisfying
    $[X,Y] = Z$ (the central generator).

    In the Weil representation $\mathbf{3}$, the character values on generators are
    \begin{equation}
      \chi_{\mathbf{3}}(X^{\pm 1}) = 1,
      \qquad
      \chi_{\mathbf{3}}(Y^{\pm 1}) = \omega^{\pm 1},
      \qquad
      \chi_{\mathbf{3}}((XY)^{\pm 1}) = \omega^{\pm 1},
      \label{eq:delta27-chars}
    \end{equation}
    where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$.
    The adjacency eigenvalue formula~\eqref{eq:mu-formula-2I} then gives
    \begin{equation}
      \mu_{\mathbf{3}}
      = \frac{1}{3}\bigl(1 + 1 + \omega + \omega^{-1} + \omega + \omega^{-1}\bigr)
      = \frac{1}{3}\bigl(2 + 2(\omega + \bar\omega)\bigr)
      = \frac{1}{3}(2 - 2) = 0,
      \label{eq:delta27-mu0}
    \end{equation}
    using $\omega + \bar\omega = 2\cos(2\pi/3) = -1$.
    Hence $\lambda_{\mathbf{3}} = 6$.

    \subsubsection{Proto-hierarchy on $\Delta(27)$}

    \begin{equation}

      = 2\cos\!\tfrac{2\pi a}{3} + 2\cos\!\tfrac{2\pi b}{3}
      + 2\cos\!\tfrac{2\pi(a+b)}{3}.
      \label{eq:delta27-abelian-mu}
    \end{equation}
    For $(a,b) \in \{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,0),(0,2),(2,2)\}$ one finds $\mu_{a,b} = 0$,
    so $\lambda_{a,b} = 6$: these abelian sectors are degenerate with the Weil pair.
    The maximum non-trivial eigenvalue occurs at $(a,b)=(1,2)$ and its permutations, giving
    $\mu_{1,2} = -3$ and $\lambda_{1,2} = 9$.

    The admissibility contrast at the $\Delta(27)$ level is therefore
    \begin{equation}

      = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\mathbf{3}}}}

      = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}

    characters share $\lambda = 6$ with the Weil representation.

    three-dimensional sector over all competitors only emerges at the $\Sigma(168)$ level,

        of any non-identity element is the whole group).
        \item[(iii)] \textbf{Schur simplicity.}

        $\mu_\rho = |C_4|\,\chi_\rho(C_4)/\dim\rho$.

    the character table) is $\{i, -i, 1\}$, giving $\chi_{\mathbf{3}}(g) = i + (-i) + 1 = 1$.

    This identity plays the same structural role as:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item the Pauli tracelessness $\chi_{\rho_4}(s) = 0$ on $Q_8$, which placed the
      quaternionic sector at the \emph{centre} of the adjacency spectrum ($\mu = 0$);
      \item the golden-ratio identity $\varphi - 1/\varphi = 1$ on $2I$, which fixed
      $\mu_{\chi_2} = 6$ and established the spin tower.
    \end{itemize}
    In the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ case, the identity $\chi_{\mathbf{3}}(g) = 1$ places the
    fundamental sector strictly above centre ($\mu_{\mathbf{3}} = 14 > 0$), giving it the
    smallest non-trivial Laplacian eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathbf{3}} = 28 < d$.

\section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

  This note has carried out the spectral admissibility programme on two parallel
  inclusion chains,
  \begin{equation}
    Q_8 \;\subset\; 2I \;\subset\; \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)
    \;\xrightarrow{\;p,q\to\infty\;}
    \mathrm{SU}(2)
    \label{eq:chain-conclusion}
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta(27) \;\subset\; \Sigma(168) \;\subset\; \mathrm{SU}(3),
    \label{eq:chain-su3-conclusion}
  \end{equation}
  producing six families of results at progressively larger scales.

  \paragraph{Steps 1--3 ($Q_8$).}
    On the Cayley graph $\Gamma(Q_8, \{\pm i, \pm j, \pm k\})$, the
    bounded-flux constraint $|\partial_t\chi_v| \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ induces the
    admissibility envelope $A^{\max}_n = c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.
    The quaternionic non-abelian sector $\rho_4$ carries the smallest
    non-trivial eigenvalue $\lambda_{\rho_4} = 6$, giving it the largest
    admissibility window by a factor of $\sqrt{4/3} \approx 1.155$ over the
    abelian sectors ($\lambda = 8$).
    This hierarchy is exact, non-perturbative, and confirmed by the exact
    one-mode DBI analysis.

  \paragraph{Step 4 (algebraic origin).}
    The factor $\sqrt{4/3}$ is not accidental.
    It originates in the Pauli tracelessness identity
    $\chi_{\rho_4}(s) = 0$ for all $s \in S$, which forces $\mu_{\rho_4} = 0$
    and places the quaternionic sector at the centre of the adjacency spectrum.
    This is a structural consequence of the embedding $Q_8 \hookrightarrow
\mathrm{SU}(2)$, not a numerical coincidence.

  \paragraph{Step 5 ($2I$).}
    On the Cayley graph of the binary icosahedral group with generating set
    $S = 10a \cup 10b$, the hierarchy resolves into a four-level spin tower:
    \begin{equation}
      \lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2} = 18
      \;<\; \lambda_1 = 20
      \;<\; \lambda_2 = 24
      \;<\; \lambda_{5/2} = 28.
      \label{eq:2I-ordering-concl}
    \end{equation}
    The admissibility contrast increases to $\sqrt{14/9} \approx 1.247$, and
    its algebraic origin is the golden-ratio identity
    $\varphi - 1/\varphi = 1$.
    Both the Q8 and 2I results are instances of the same mechanism:
    the generating set $S$ is positively correlated with the low-spin
    representations in character, giving $\mu_{\text{low}} > \mu_{\text{high}}$
    and hence $\lambda_{\text{low}} < \lambda_{\text{high}}$.

  \paragraph{Steps 6a--6d (LPS family).}
    The Lubotzky--Phillips--Sarnak construction provides, for every prime
    $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, an explicit symmetric generating set $S_p \subset
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)$ of cardinality $p+1$ defined by the
    four-square decomposition of $p$.
    The resulting Cayley graphs $X^{p,q}$ are Ramanujan, and their spectral
    measure converges to the $S^3$ Laplacian as $q \to \infty$.

    The character computation in the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ limit shows that the
    spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ sector retains the smallest non-trivial Laplacian
    eigenvalue for every prime $p$ examined, establishing the hierarchy
    \begin{equation}
      \lambda_{1/2} < \lambda_j \quad \forall\, j > \tfrac{1}{2}
      \label{eq:lps-hierarchy-concl}
    \end{equation}
    as a persistent structural feature of the LPS generating mechanism.
    The admissibility ratio satisfies
    \begin{equation}
      1 \;<\; R_p \;\leq\; \frac{\sqrt{p}+1}{\sqrt{p}-1}
      \;\xrightarrow{p\to\infty}\; 1,
      \label{eq:Rp-concl}
    \end{equation}
    where the upper bound is the Ramanujan constraint and the lower bound
    reflects Proposition~\ref{prop:lps-hierarchy}.

  \paragraph{Step~7 ($\Delta(27)$ and $\Sigma(168)$).}
    The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ chain produces exact parallels of Steps~4 and~5.
    On $\Delta(27) = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{F}_3)$, the character sum
    $\mu_{\mathbf{3}} = 0$ establishes the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ structural anchor via
    the Weil tracelessness identity; however, the hierarchy is degenerate (six abelian
    sectors share $\lambda = 6$ with the Weil pair, giving $R_{\Delta(27)} =
    \sqrt{3/2} \approx 1.225$ against the worst abelian sector only).
    On $\Sigma(168) = \mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$, the character identity
    $\chi_{\mathbf{3}}(g) = 1$ for all $g \in C_4$ fixes $\lambda_{\mathbf{3}} = 28$ and
    yields the strict four-level hierarchy
    \begin{equation}
      \lambda_{\mathbf{3}} = \lambda_{\bar{\mathbf{3}}} = 28
      \;<\;
      \lambda_{\mathbf{6}} = \lambda_{\mathbf{8}} = 42
      \;<\;
      \lambda_{\mathbf{7}} = 48,
      \label{eq:sigma168-ordering-concl}
    \end{equation}
    with admissibility contrast $R_{\Sigma(168)} = \sqrt{12/7} \approx 1.309$.
    The fundamental and anti-fundamental representations $\mathbf{3}$ and $\bar{\mathbf{3}}$
    are co-admissible and strictly dominate all competitors, in exact analogy with
    $\lambda_{1/2} = \lambda_{3/2}$ on $2I$.

  \paragraph{The full picture.}
    The admissibility ratio evolves along the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ chain as
    \begin{equation}
      \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{4}{3}}}_{\displaystyle Q_8}
      \;\longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{14}{9}}}_{\displaystyle 2I}
      \;\longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{R_p \in (1,\,(\sqrt{p}+1)/(\sqrt{p}-1))}_{\displaystyle
      \mathrm{LPS}}
      \;\longrightarrow\;
      1,
      \label{eq:ratio-sequence-concl}
    \end{equation}
    and along the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ chain as
    \begin{equation}
      \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}}\;(\text{degen.})}_{\displaystyle \Delta(27)}
      \;\longrightarrow\;
      \underbrace{\sqrt{\tfrac{12}{7}}}_{\displaystyle \Sigma(168)}
      \;\longrightarrow\;
      [\text{open}],
      \label{eq:ratio-sequence-su3-concl}
    \end{equation}
    where the final entry corresponds to an LPS-type family for $\mathrm{SU}(3)$
    (open; analogous to Sections~\ref{sec:step6a-lps}--\ref{sec:step6d}).
    Both sequences satisfy $R > 1$ at every finite level and are expected to tend to $1$
    in the respective continuum limits $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$.

  \paragraph{Interpretation.}
    The sequence~\eqref{eq:ratio-sequence-concl} has a precise physical
    reading: the bounded-flux filter is strongest for the smallest,
    most discrete substrates, and weakens as the substrate grows toward the
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ continuum.
    In the limit, the full rotational symmetry of $S^3$ is restored and
    every spin sector becomes equally admissible.
    The discrete hierarchy is not a defect of the finite approximations
    but a genuine structural feature of bounded relational capacity at
    finite resolution.

    The algebraic origin of the hierarchy is uniform across both chains.
    At every level, the generating set $S$ has higher character coherence
    with the lowest-dimensional faithful representations than with the higher ones.
    This coherence, measured by $\kappa_j = \mu_j/d$ (with $d = |S|$), is the
    fundamental observable driving the admissibility selection.

  \paragraph{Summary.}
    The analysis demonstrates that, on every Cayley graph in the chains
    $Q_8 \subset 2I \subset \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)$ and
    $\Delta(27) \subset \Sigma(168) \subset \mathrm{SU}(3)$,
    the local bounded-flux constraint selects the lowest-dimensional non-abelian sector
    as the most admissible.
    This selection is purely spectral: it requires no additional dynamical
    principle beyond the constraint itself and the algebraic structure of the
    generating set.
    The mechanism survives the non-linear DBI correction on $Q_8$, persists
    through the explicit spin tower of $2I$, extends to the full LPS family
    approaching $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and now admits an exact parallel for the
    $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ chain through $\Sigma(168)$.
    It constitutes a minimal but complete demonstration that bounded
    relational capacity can favour low-dimensional non-abelian sectors through
    spectral admissibility alone, uniformly across the gauge structure of the
    Standard Model.
